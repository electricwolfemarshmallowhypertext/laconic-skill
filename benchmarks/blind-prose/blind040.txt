Because lunar months are shorter than the Gregorian calendar months, using a lunar calendar exclusively can make scheduling appointments and events more difficult. For example, if a lunar calendar is used to plan a meeting or appointment, it may be difficult to schedule the event on the same day each month, as the lunar month shifts every year by approximately 11 days. This can make it difficult to coordinate schedules in advance.

Additionally, because the length of each lunar month can vary, there can be some uncertainty around when holidays or other important events will occur. For example, in the Islamic calendar, the exact timing of the start and end of Ramadan can vary by a day or two each year, based on the sighting of the new moon.

Despite these practical challenges, many religious communities continue to use lunar calendars for religious purposes because they are deeply tied to cultural and spiritual traditions. However, for practical purposes, a blend of lunar and solar-based calendars, such as what is commonly used in Muslim practice, can offer a more stable and predictable scheduling system.
