Certainly, here are some examples of widely-read books that make effective use of foreshadowing:

1. "Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone" by J.K. Rowling - The author uses foreshadowing to hint at the identity of the main villain, Voldemort, throughout the book, building up to the dramatic reveal at the end.

2. "Jane Eyre" by Charlotte Bronte - The author uses foreshadowing to create a dark and ominous atmosphere, hinting at the dark secrets of the characters and the potential danger that lurks beneath the surface.

3. "The Great Gatsby" by F. Scott Fitzgerald - The author uses foreshadowing to create a sense of tragedy and foreboding, hinting at the doomed nature of the main character's pursuit of his dreams.

4. "To Kill a Mockingbird" by Harper Lee - The author uses foreshadowing to create suspense and tension, hinting at the conflict that looms between the innocent protagonist and the forces of prejudice and injustice in her world.

5. "The Lord of the Flies" by William Golding - The author uses foreshadowing to create a sense of impending doom and tragedy, hinting at the violent and chaotic events that will unfold in the near future.
