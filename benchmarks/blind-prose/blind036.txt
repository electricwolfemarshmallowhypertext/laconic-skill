The Islamic calendar, also known as the Hijri calendar, is based on the lunar cycle and is used in Muslim practice to determine the dates of religious observances, including Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, and Eid al-Adha. It is significant because it establishes the dates for several important religious events and helps Muslims keep track of the lunar months throughout the year. Additionally, it serves as a reminder of important events in Islamic history, such as the migration of Prophet Muhammad from Mecca to Medina (known as the Hijra), which marks the beginning of the Islamic calendar. The Islamic calendar has 12 months, each lasting either 29 or 30 days, and it is used in everyday life for scheduling religious and social events.
