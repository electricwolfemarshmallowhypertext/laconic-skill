Sure, here are additional modifications for individuals with specific injuries:

For lower back pain:
1. Avoid exercises that involve bending forward, such as cable crunches, cable woodchops, and cable rotations.
2. Instead, focus on exercises that involve standing upright or lying on your back, such as lat pulldowns, chest presses, and leg presses.
3. Decrease the weight used on the machine and focus on proper form and control.
4. Add exercises that target the core muscles without putting undue stress on the lower back, such as planks and side planks.

For knee pain:
1. Avoid exercises that put stress on the knees, such as leg presses, cable pulls from the knees, and squats.
2. Instead, focus on exercises that target the upper body, such as chest presses, lat pulldowns, and cable rows.
3. Modify leg exercises by using lighter weights and performing exercises in partial ranges of motion.
4. Add exercises that strengthen the muscles surrounding the knee joint, such as hip abductions, calf raises, and leg extensions (using light weights).

Always consult with a medical professional before starting any new exercise program, and listen to your body during each exercise. Adjust the resistance and range of motion to suit your individual needs and abilities.
